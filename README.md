
# DevOps

The core mission of DevOps is to deliver better software, faster and more reliably, by making development and operations work as one team and automating the path from idea to running system.

DevOps is not a tool, technology, or framework. It is more a philosophy, concept and a methodology. It is a set of practices and tools that combines and automates software development (Dev) and IT operations (Ops) to improve and shorten the development life cycle.

Proposals to combine software development with deployment and operations began to appear in the late 80s and early 90s. The motivations for what has become modern DevOps and several standard DevOps practices such as automated build and test, continuous integration, and continuous delivery originated in the Agile world, which dates (informally) to the 1990s, and formally to 2001. Today, DevOps focuses on the deployment of developed software.

At its core DevOps is the collaboration between the software development and operations teams. DevOps emphasis is on the automation of software development processes like build, test, incident detection and response, release, and others to yield a faster time-to-market, high-quality products, and reduced failures and rollbacks of software/features.

### Main Objectives of DevOps:

**1. Deliver software faster**

The biggest goal is to get new features, fixes, and improvements into the hands of users much quicker.
Instead of waiting months for a big release, teams aim for frequent, small updates (sometimes multiple times a day).

**2. Improve collaboration**

Break down the traditional wall between developers and operations people.
Everyone shares the same goals, tools, and responsibility for the product — from writing code all the way to keeping it running in production.

**3. Increase reliability and quality**

Faster does not mean lower quality.
DevOps focuses on building systems that are stable, can recover quickly from problems, and have fewer bugs by catching issues early through testing and monitoring.

**4. Automate as much as possible**

Manual, repetitive work (building, testing, deploying, checking servers) is slow and error-prone.
Automation reduces human mistakes and frees people to do more valuable work.

**5. Get continuous feedback**

Create short feedback loops so teams quickly learn what works and what doesn’t — from automated tests, monitoring tools, and real users.
This helps teams improve constantly instead of waiting for big failures.

**6. Reduce risk**

By releasing small changes often and having good monitoring + rollback plans, the impact of any single change is much smaller.
Problems are easier to find and fix.

**7. Focus on the customer**

Ultimately, all of the above serve one purpose: deliver value to users faster and more reliably so the business can respond quickly to needs and stay competitive.
